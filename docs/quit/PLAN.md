# Off weed — the plan

Written Tuesday, Oct 6, 2026. Day 0. Home to Toronto Thursday, reading week, back in London after.

This is built from four research passes (clinical literature, r/leaves and other
ex-user communities, medication/body interactions, behaviour-change science) and

## The one-paragraph version

You are quitting a very high THC load (a 1 g live-resin cart every 48–72 h is
roughly 280–425 mg THC a day — several times what "heavy flower" means in the
studies). Expect withdrawal at the long end of every timeline: evenings hard for
3 days, worst sleep and mood days 4–7, appetite back in 1–2 weeks, dreams and
sleep normal in 3–6 weeks, "nothing is wrong but I feel flat" into week 4. None
of it is dangerous. All of it is temporary. The job is not to feel good this
week. The job is to eat on a schedule, move once a day, protect sleep, not
drink much, and make it to each evening with the pen already gone. Reading week
at your parents' is the easiest possible place to do the hard part. The return
to London (day ~10–14) is the actual test, so you plan for it now.

## Today (Tuesday, day 0)

1. **The cart and the battery leave the house.** Not a drawer. Gone. Keeping one
   "in case" is the most common relapse setup in every community thread.
2. **Open the app → Recovery → Start the quit.** Set "last hit", write your reasons
   in your own words. Decide the day 3 and day 7 rewards now, not later.
3. **Tell one person today.** One sentence: "I'm off weed for a bit, starting
   today." Pick whoever you will actually be honest with. Parents on Thursday.
4. **Eat a real dinner.** Order it if you have to. Protein.
5. **Switch what the nicotine goes in.** Keep the nicotine (one quit at a time),
   but the vape is the same gesture, same pocket, same cue as the cart. Pouches
   or gum this week if you can get them; if not, at least no new vape when this
   one dies for 30 days.
6. **Lemborexant as prescribed, with a light snack, not a big meal.** Big meal
   early (6–7 pm), pill later. Phone out of the bedroom. Dreams will be strange
   tonight or tomorrow. That is the plan working.

## Wednesday (day 1)

- Morning: Vyvanse right after a big protein breakfast (25–35 g). Eating before
  the dose is the only window where your appetite is on your side.
- Set alarms: eat at 9, 1, 5, 9. Hunger cues are off (Vyvanse + withdrawal); the
  need for food is not. A shake counts.
- One hard, sweaty session. 30 minutes is enough. This is the single most
  repeated piece of advice from people who did it, and the only thing with
  trial data on cannabis cravings.
- Evening is the window. Hot shower, bed early, one "craving" log if it hits.
  You are not fighting it, you are timing it.
- Daily check-in in the app before bed (under a minute).

## Thursday (day 2) — travel home

- Eat before the train/drive. Pack snacks. Low blood sugar on day 2 reads as
  rage and craving.
- Tell your parents the plan in the car or at dinner. Script below. Ask for one
  concrete thing: **dinner together every night this week.** That is the single
  highest-value ask (scheduled eating, accountability, company at the time you
  would normally smoke).
- Rearrange nothing yet; you are not in the smoke environment, which is the
  point.

## Reading week (days 2–9) — the easy part, used well

- **Fixed wake time, every day, including weekends.** Light outside within an
  hour. This is what drags sleep back faster than anything else.
- **Four meals a day by the clock.** Toronto has your mom's kitchen. Use it.
  Calorie-dense, protein-forward, iron-rich (red meat, eggs, lentils, spinach
  with something acidic; keep coffee and dairy an hour away from iron).
- **Move daily.** Gym, hockey, golf with your dad, long walks. Two or three
  proper sessions plus walks beats forcing four hard lifts on no food. Nothing
  about this week is a performance.
- **No bars this week.** Not a sobriety rule, a sequencing rule. Alcohol does
  the opposite of every job you need done (sleep, appetite, steady mood,
  judgment) and heavy drinking during a weed quit is the documented path back
  to a hit. One or two drinks with dinner at home is a different thing.
- **Expect days 4–7 to be the worst.** Angry outbursts and trouble falling
  asleep are the two most distressing items in the clinical data. Mood will
  dip and feel like depression. It is withdrawal chemistry on top of venlafaxine
  side effects (which overlap heavily). Do not evaluate your life this week.
  Do not text her this week.
- **Before you leave Toronto:** text the one or two friends in London who
  smoke around you. Not a speech. "Off it for a while, don't pass me anything."
  Decide which friends you are seeing first week back (the non-smoking ones).

## Back in London (days ~10–21) — the actual test

This lands exactly in the window where people relapse because they feel fine.
Day 10–14 the brain says "see, you're good, one hit won't hurt." It is the
single most-reported relapse point in the communities and matches the
clinical "symptoms fade days 7–14" curve.

- **Before you unpack, change the room.** Move one piece of furniture, bin the
  stash spot, clean the surface where the pen lived. Autopilot fires on
  unchanged environments.
- **Bars: pre-decide or don't go.** For the first month, if you go: eat a real
  dinner first, max 3 drinks decided before you leave, no pre-drink to "calm
  down", a leave-by time. If you need a drink to be able to walk in, that is
  the anxiety talking and tonight is a skip. Delilah's will be there next week.
- **The refusal line is one sentence.** "I'm off it for a bit." No story. Then go
  get a water. Over-explaining invites the debate.
- **A slip is a slip.** If it happens: log it in the app in 30 seconds, do not
  turn it into a night, tomorrow is a clean day. Early slips are the strongest
  predictor of full relapse, but only when they turn into "well, the streak's
  gone anyway." The app shows clean-days-percent beside the streak for exactly
  this reason.
- **Week 3–4 will feel pointlessly bad.** Physical symptoms are gone, so the
  flatness feels like a verdict instead of a phase. It is a phase. Energy and
  motivation come back in steps. Keep logging; the trend line is the proof.

## The month after

- Sleep and dreams normal around weeks 4–6. Receptor density back to roughly
  normal around day 28.
- Then, and only then, taper the nicotine. Sleep problems in withdrawal are
  exactly what drives people to vape more; adding a nicotine cold-turkey in
  week 1 is how people fail both.
- Day 30, 60, 90 rewards. Spend the cart money on something you can hold.

## Rules of the toolkit (in the app)

- **Craving now** → rate it, HALT check, pick a tool, 10-minute timer, re-rate.
  You will see the number drop. Average craving lasts 10–30 minutes.
- **Check in** → nightly, under a minute. Weekly, do the full 19-item scale.
- **If → then** → your pre-decided moves. Read them when you are not craving so
  they are there when you are.
- **People & lines** → Mom, Dad, Sunil, your doctor, and three 24/7 numbers
  (988, Reach Out 519-433-2023, ConnexOntario 1-866-531-2600). Thoughts of
  hurting yourself, panic that will not settle, or three nights of no sleep:
  call, do not deliberate.
- **Milestones** → day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90. Rewards decided up front and
  only claimed clean. This is the best-evidenced behavioural lever for cannabis
  specifically (contingency management), self-applied.

## Red flags → doctor same day

- Thoughts of self-harm, or hopelessness that is getting worse, not better, past day 7.
- New paranoia, hallucinations, or feeling disorganized (rare, but documented after abrupt heavy cessation).
- Vomiting you cannot stop, especially if hot showers are the only relief (cannabinoid hyperemesis can show up right after stopping).
- Weight still falling at the end of week 2, or more than 2–3 kg down.
- Three nights in a row of essentially no sleep.
